# The Journey — first playable chapter

## Diagnosis before revising the interrupted Phase 3 work
The deployed baseline is Phase 2 (a63a8b7); Phase 3 changes are local and unfinished.
- Movement: existing collision-tested click routes, WASD, follow camera; local run/jump/joystick added. Keyboard velocity still starts abruptly; improve without weakening collision.
- NPCs: local state machines, walking/grazing/looking and prototype human models. No production hero models.
- Missions: new local durable AVAILABLE/ACTIVE/COMPLETED framework with prerequisite-based LOCKED. Five physical sheep can be delivered in a CPU simulation. One sheep delivered through real browser controls; full browser playthrough not yet passed.
- Weakness: proximity auto-follow feels like collecting tokens. Uninterrupted running and pushing into sheep have too little consequence. Five similar trips can become a chore.
- Stories: 33 own Korean summaries and Bethlehem timeline scenes already work. Mission outcome must not cause scripture events. Freely accessible stories stay available.
- Continuity: eleven free-selection worlds are in biblical episode order but have little connective framing. Reuse the order; add journal questions and subsequent destination.
- Records: story progress/notes/audio settings already persist under separate keys. New adventure state must not overwrite those keys; replay retains achievement history.
- Sound: five locally authored WAV tracks and synth ambience; no recorded narration. Reuse these, add modest original feedback cues.
- Visuals: architecture has five structural types; characters are prototypes and remain visibly below final art target.
- Mobile: previous emulation ~30 FPS; added flock/controls require new measurements, not assumed performance.
- Return motive: persist a calm assembled flock and a discovered route; journal connects later chapters back to earlier questions. No streak rewards or economy.

## Scope: two actions, one connected chapter
1. Read animals: approach at walking pace, wait outside their personal space, then guide at a comfortable distance. Rushing/startling and crowding produce visible retreat; a wall breaks sight. An open-sided pen tests choosing the exit, rather than pulling through a wall.
2. Read the place: footprints and warm light suggest a narrow stone-wall path. Crossing it reveals a vista and records an optional return destination on the map.
The flock completion → shepherd account → warm alley/birth account → Nazareth is an editorial invitation, not a causal sequence. Scripture scenes remain optional and replayable before or after the invented task.
A separate resident errand and more missions are deliberately deferred until this slice receives human feedback.

## Intended first-session pacing (design hypothesis, not measured human fun)
0–2 min: movement, meet shepherd, learn the first animal's distance cues.
2–7 min: guide the flock, choose the pen exit, choose whether to group animals or make shorter trips.
7–10+ min: optional narrow path / vista, shepherd and birth accounts, journal and Nazareth invitation.
No timers or artificial waiting to manufacture a ten-minute duration. A skilled/automated player can finish sooner.

## Existing world order, preserved
Bethlehem → Nazareth → Jordan → Galilee → Sermon on the Mount → Feeding the five thousand → Jericho → Jerusalem → Gethsemane → Golgotha → Empty tomb.
These are selected gospel episodes, not a claim to depict every event or a harmonized day-by-day chronology.

## Human playtest gate
Ask a first-time player to play without instructions beyond the UI. Observe whether they notice cautious/calm/startled sheep, find an exit, recover a waiting animal, notice the changed flock, distinguish invented play from scripture, and choose a next destination. Record confusion, repeated failures and voluntary exploration. Automated completion only establishes functionality, not enjoyment or retention.
